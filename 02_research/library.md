# PTG Research Library

**How to read this file**
- **Check** = how well the claim has been checked.
  READ = text pulled from the PDF in this folder. SNIPPET = search summary only, open the source before relying on it.
  NOT READ = the file is in this folder but nobody has gone through it yet.
- **Loop** = which part of the PTG system the source supports (see the system map at the bottom).
- One rule for every shelf: a source can justify a **hypothesis to test**, not a claim about a specific player.

## Shelf 1: Transfer and representative design (why empty-gym practice doesn't carry over)

| Perceptual-cognitive training review 2026, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13642762/ | Evidence doesn't establish durable decision gains or transfer to competition. | SNIPPET (from another chat) | Keep B3 "transfer proof" UNKNOWN |

## Shelf 1b: Seeing the read (Film to Floor: freeze-frames and viewpoint)

| Source | What it says | Check | Loop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Müller & Morris-Binelli, temporal occlusion training meta-analysis (2024), https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11467115/ | Freezing video before the outcome (occlusion) gave a **large** training effect, and it carried over to **both video and field** transfer tests equally. Sport in general, not basketball only. | SNIPPET | The strongest support for "pause before the decision." Note: it trains *anticipation* (reading an opponent's movement), which isn't the same as *tactical* decisions |
| Roca & Williams (2017), transferability of decision-making, https://research.stmarys.ac.uk/id/eprint/1537/ | Some decision skill transfers between similar sports (soccer players did better on basketball occlusion items than on tennis items). | SNIPPET | Reads may share structure, which supports held-out clips |
| Kittel et al. (2020), 360° VR vs. broadcast video, RCT, 32 umpires, https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/24733938.2020.1754449 | 5 weeks: the 360° VR group improved decisions. The **broadcast-video group showed no significant change.** | SNIPPET | Supports the viewpoint hypothesis (broadcast seat ≠ player's seat). Umpires, not players |
| Softball umpires, 360° VR vs. 2D broadcast (2024), https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2024.1368648/full | **No accuracy difference.** VR rated more engaging and game-like. | SNIPPET | Counter-evidence: viewpoint is UNKNOWN, not settled. Test it yourself |
| Frontiers 2025, progressive occlusion + ball-trajectory anticipation, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1547493/full | Progressive occlusion with a control group, in a team set-piece setting (likely soccer). | SNIPPET | A design pattern for progressive pause points |
| Video-based QB training (2020), https://library.usi.edu/record/1416660/files/Powless%20et%20al.%202020.pdf | No clear improvement overall. | SNIPPET | A null result to keep in view |

**What this shelf changes:** freeze-frame film study has **better** evidence than plain "decision video training." That holds when it trains reading the opponent and when it gets tested in a new context. Viewpoint is a real open question with mixed results.

## Shelf 2: Attention and cues (what to say to the player)

| Source | What it says | Check | Loop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fatigue × attentional focus, free throws (2025), https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12517065/ | 30 players. External focus 75% vs. internal 65% under fatigue. Immediate performance, **not retention**. | SNIPPET | One Cue variable: internal vs. external |
| Fatigue and focus review (Frontiers 2025), https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1760814/pdf | External focus partly protected shooting under fatigue. Preliminary. | SNIPPET | Fatigue checkpoint |
| Rezaei et al., focus + modeling, free throws, https://brieflands.com/articles/intjssh-135272.pdf | Combining attentional focus with modeling helped acquisition and retention in beginners. Year unconfirmed. | SNIPPET | Retention test design |

| Han et al. (2025), quiet eye meta-analysis, *Applied Sciences*, https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/7/3871 | Longer quiet eye duration correlates with shooting ability (r = 0.67) across levels. **Correlational.** | SNIPPET | Gaze is a candidate feature; don't claim it causes makes |
| Sirnik et al. (2022), visual attention in shooting review, https://www.iat.uni-leipzig.de/datenbanken/iks/dbb/Record/4083203 | 26 studies: QE training *or* traditional training can improve shooting; longer QE and fewer fixations go with better shots. | SNIPPET | QE training = a valid experiment arm |
| Mizusaki et al. (2025), QE variability, 26 players × 100 free throws | Longer QE may help by **reducing variability** of QE. | SNIPPET | Consistency of routine, not just length |
| Aiken, Fairbrother & Post (2012), self-controlled video feedback, set shot, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3438820/ | Letting the learner **choose when** to see video feedback helped learn technique. | SNIPPET | Player-controlled replay, not constant replay |

**Gap found:** no 2025–26 meta-analysis pools **retention** for external vs. internal focus in basketball. PTG's own delayed retests would be new evidence.

## System map these shelves serve

```
INNER LOOP (days):   design cue block → log → compare inside noise band → keep/drop    [Shelves 2, 3]
OUTER LOOP (weeks):  lesson → delayed retest → game check → revise model                [Shelves 1, 4]
SENSOR:              Auditor: how game-like was practice?                               [Shelf 1]
DISTURBANCE TEST:    fatigue checkpoint                                                 [Shelves 2, 4]
YOUR LOOP:           predict → see → compare → judgment log                             [Shelf 6]
GOAL:                shrink the practice-to-game gap (not hours, not liking)
OPEN UNKNOWN:        does practice-side change transfer to games? (B3, every run so far)
```
