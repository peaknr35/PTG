# Your Goal, Mapped as a System

## 1. Read the goal like an engineer reads a requirement
| Part | Type | What it really asks for |
|---|---|---|
| "Systems that help **businesses** profit and save time" | **The product** | A buyer with a costly, recurring problem |
| "My love of building" | **The engine** | Your energy source. Protect it |
| "Meaningful work, wealth" | **Outcomes** | Revenue that doesn't scale 1:1 with your hours |
| "Freedom to travel, be present for family" | **Constraints** | Hour cap, low dependence on you, location-free delivery |
| "Help others" | **Purpose** | Who benefits beyond the buyer |

**The key systems point:** freedom and family aren't the *reward at the end*. They're **constraints from day one.** A business that hits the money goal by eating every evening has failed the requirement.

## 2. The stocks (what has to pile up)
- **Cash / runway**: buys time to build
- **Proof**: case studies with real numbers ("saved 6 hrs/week")
- **Reusable systems**: templates, ICM kits, contracts, scripts you sell more than once
- **Relationships**: people who know you solve problems
- **Your hours**: fixed at about 168 a week. The scarcest stock
- **Skill**: systems thinking + design + delivery (what you're building now)

## 3. The loops
```
R1  Deliver value → proof (case study) → trust → more clients → more value     (the growth engine)
R2  Reuse systems → each delivery costs less time → more margin AND more free hours   (the freedom engine)
B1  More clients → more hours → hits the HOUR CAP → stop taking custom work     (the guardrail; must be designed in)
B2  Burnout / family strain → less energy → worse work → fewer clients          (what happens if B1 is missing)
```
- **R1 without R2 = a job you created.** Revenue rises with hours, and hours eat family time.
- **R2 is the leverage loop.** Every system you build should be built **to be reused**, the way your ICM kits already are.

## 4. The traps to watch
| Trap | How it would show up |
|---|---|
| **Shifting the burden** | Doing custom work forever instead of turning it into reusable systems |
| **Seeking the wrong goal** | Chasing revenue or cool tech instead of revenue **per hour** and free time |
| **KNOW without DO** (your film-study trap) | Endless research and blueprints, no customer conversations |
| **Success to the successful** | One big client takes all your time and starves the rest |

## 5. Where PTG fits (hard truth)
- Your goal names **businesses** as the customer. PTG, as designed, serves **players**. That's a mismatch.
- **Three honest options:**
  1. **PTG = your learning lab and portfolio.** It's where you master systems thinking, design and AI delivery on a problem you love. The money comes from building systems for businesses. **This fits the goal best right now.**
  2. **PTG goes B2B:** trainers, academies or high school programs pay to save film-review time and keep clients. This is a hypothesis to test with interviews, not to build around.
  3. **PTG stays a passion project** next to the business. That's fine too, but don't plan your income around it.
- Whichever you choose, everything you've built transfers: the foundation (graded evidence), the glossary (shared language), the gates (verify before trusting), the event log (nothing vanishes), and APIV. **That *is* the "systems that help businesses" skill set.**

## 5b. What "portfolio" means concretely
A portfolio isn't "an app I built." It's **proof that you can turn a messy problem into a system that's correct, and show how you know.** PTG already has the raw material:

| Portfolio piece | What it shows a business buyer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **"How I audit AI-generated systems"**: the 6+ AI versions you caught (invented thresholds, misused papers, definition drift, the normalized-coordinate bug, the missing import) | You don't ship confident fiction. **This is rare, and buyers fear AI slop** | Material exists in this chat; write it up as a case study |
| **The method kit**: foundation (graded evidence), glossary, gates, append-only event log, APIV, requirement ledger | A repeatable way to build any system, not one-off work | Exists as PTG files → turn into **blank templates** |
| **Rehearsal-01 results**: predictions vs. reality, definitions v0 → v1 | You test before you build, and you show the data | After you run it |
| **Before/after**: 03_design/boards/v1-invented-formulas.html (invented formulas) → 03_design/boards/v2-honest-model.html (honest model) | You can spot the difference and fix it | Exists |
| **A one-page "Practice-to-Game Lab" story**: lived problem → system → evidence → what you learned | You can explain systems to non-technical people | To write |

**The rule (R2, the freedom loop):** every PTG artifact gets a **blank, reusable version** for business clients. The basketball glossary becomes "a glossary template," and the foundation becomes "an evidence-graded rules template."
